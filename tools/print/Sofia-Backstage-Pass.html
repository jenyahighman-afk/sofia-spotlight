Part 3

The big goal, in small steps

The big goal

Dance your solo at the Winter Showcase from memory, full out, eyes up, holding the ending to the very last note. And love doing it.

Four parts get you there, one after the other. Don't skip ahead.

You are here1

Own it

Sep 28 – Nov 8

Know every step by heart. No video, nobody to follow. Memory + knees: no new tricks yet.

You are here2

Clean it

Nov 9 – Nov 29

Make it sharp: feet, knees, arms, eyes. One fix at a time. Show in your head starts.

You are here3

Perform it

Nov 30 – Jan 3

Dance it like the real show. Full out, costume on, little audiences at home.

You are here4

Taper

Jan 4 – the show

Less work, more rest. Nothing new. Fresh legs, calm head, early nights.

How big goals get done

  • One 8-count at a time. Learn it, then join it to the one before.
  • One fix at a time. It's done when it shows on two videos in a row.
  • A short practice most days. 35–45 minutes at home is plenty.
  • Never more than 2 days without your solo. On busy days, the memory run counts (and on Monday, Hannah's videos and picturing it).
  • Finish lines get prizes. Your finish lines are on Today's season card and on Toolkit page 1. When you make one, tap ✓ Made it with Mom, then pick a reward and write it on Toolkit page 2b: a movie night, a new scrunchie, choosing Saturday breakfast.
  • How ready? Tap How ready? on Today to see four bars for your solo. Pause & Predict fills Know, fixes that showed fill Clean, your eight tricks fill Tricks, full runs and Sunday films fill Perform.

Part 5

Keep all four spotlights on

A happy dancer has four lights. Each one gets some time every week, and none of them should go dark.

Dance

Classes plus short home practices. That's enough. More isn't always better.

School

Homework first on studio days. Read a little every day. Ask for help early, before it piles up.

Home

Chores are teamwork: pack your dance bag and put it by the door, help with dinner, and tidy your dance spot in 2 minutes.

Fun

Friends, playing outside, drawing, family time, even doing nothing at all. Fun is part of the plan.

Does it feel like too much?

Always tired, grumpy about dance, a sore spot that won't go away, or worries at bedtime? Tell Mom. A lighter day is part of the plan, and the memory run is the light version.

Part 6

Time-smart tricks

  • Same time, same place. Start practice at the same time on Thu, Fri and Sun. A habit is easier than willpower.
  • Big thing first. Homework, then practice, then free time.
  • Best time on a home day: a small snack after school, dance about 45 minutes later, dinner after. Never right after a big meal: wait 1½–2 hours.
  • In the app too: on a home day the solo and the trio come first. The countdown door, the dare and the weekend games open once both are done ★. They live on Play, under Weekend games and Extras.
  • Let the timer be the boss. The app times each part. When it says done, you're done.
  • Plan on Sunday. Fill in page 6 and look at the week: tests, parties, rehearsals.
  • Pack tonight. Dance bag and water bottle by the door the night before.
  • Stuck? Do the tiniest step. Put on dance clothes and start the warm-up. Starting is the hardest part.
Part 7

Screen-smart

  • The app is a tool. Open it, practice, close it.
  • Paper first. Planning, stickers, reading your fixes and Show in your head all happen on paper or with your eyes closed.
  • Filming? The phone goes on its stand at the side, not in your hand.
  • Screens off 1 hour before bed. Phones and tablets sleep outside your room.

Screen-free swaps: draw a costume idea, read, play outside, bake with Mom, freestyle to your favorite song, build a blanket fort.

Part 8

Fuel like a dancer

Dancing burns lots of energy, and food is your fuel. Every idea here is fish-and-veggie friendly.

Every morning

Breakfast

Oatmeal with berries, eggs and toast, yogurt with granola, or a waffle with peanut butter.

1–2 hours before class

A small snack

Banana + peanut butter, apple + cheese, yogurt, crackers + hummus, or trail mix.

After class

Refuel

Chocolate milk, Greek yogurt, eggs, a tuna or salmon sandwich, a bean-and-cheese quesadilla, or edamame.

Every day

Build strong

A rainbow of fruits and veggies. Milk, yogurt or cheese for strong bones. Beans, lentils, eggs, fish and spinach for energy.

Water bottle at every class. Sip before, during and after. Pale yellow pee means you're good. Dark means drink more.

Never skip a meal. Dancers run on fuel. Growing bodies change and need lots of food. Strong dancers eat well. On show day, eat foods you know and nothing new.

Part 9

Sleep is secret practice

9–12 hours every night

That's what kids your age need. While you sleep, your brain saves the steps you practiced, and your muscles repair and grow.

so lights out by 9:00 pm for 10 hours.
  • Same bedtime every night, weekends too.
  • Late studio nights (Mon, Tue): dinner, the memory run on Tuesday, shower, bed. Homework is already done.
  • Wind down: shower, PJs, a paper book, lights out.
  • Show in your head in bed is optional. It never makes bedtime later.
Part 10

Take care of your dancer body

Your body is your instrument. Here's how to tell normal sore from stop-and-tell.

Normal: keep going

Muscles that burn while you work. Tired, achy legs the next day. A stretch you can feel that doesn't hurt.

Stop and tell Mom

Sharp or stabbing pain. Pain in a knee, ankle, wrist or your back. Swelling, limping, or pain that wakes you up or lasts more than a few days.

  • Warm up first, always. Stretch only warm muscles. A stretch, never pain. No oversplits.
  • Acro on the mat or with a spotter. Kick-overs and aerials only in class.
  • Stop before you're too tired. Tired teaches your body the wrong way.
  • Knees over toes in every plié, lunge and landing.
  • Rest counts as training. No dancing at home on Monday (videos and picturing only), and the taper is rest on purpose.
  • Happy feet: toenails short, check for blisters, socks off for floor work.
  • Nervous? Smell the flower (breathe in for 4), blow out the candle (out for 6). Three times.
  • Be kind to yourself. Mistakes are how dancers learn. Say what went well first.

For Mom

Be the stage crew, not the teacher: music, timer, filming, stickers. Ask, don't correct ("What comes next?"). Praise the work, not the talent. More on Toolkit page 3.
